💚 Digital Well-being

Built-in tools in mainstream operating systems to help you manage screen time and reduce digital distractions

Modern operating systems include powerful built-in tools for managing your digital habits. You don't always need a third-party app — start with what's already on your device.

🤖 Android

Android's Digital Wellbeing feature (available on Android 9+) gives you a dashboard of your phone usage and tools to set limits.

Key Features

  • Dashboard: See how long you've used each app today and your unlock count.
  • App Timers: Set daily limits for individual apps. When the timer runs out, the app icon turns grey and opens are blocked until the next day.
  • Focus Mode: Pause distracting apps with one tap. You can schedule Focus Mode during work or study hours.
  • Bedtime Mode: Automatically apply Do Not Disturb and fade the screen to grayscale at a set time to help you wind down.
  • Do Not Disturb: Block calls, notifications, and alerts — with granular exceptions for contacts or app categories.
  • Heads Up: Reminds you to look up from your phone while walking (Pixel devices).

How to Access

  1. Open Settings
  2. Tap Digital Wellbeing & parental controls

🍎 iOS & iPadOS

Apple's Screen Time feature (available on iOS 12+) lets you see your device usage and set limits for yourself or your family.

Key Features

  • Screen Time Summary: Weekly report showing time spent in each app and category, pickups, and notifications received.
  • App Limits: Set daily time limits by app or app category (e.g., Social Networking). When the limit is reached, a one-minute warning appears and then the app is blocked.
  • Downtime: Schedule a period (e.g., 10 PM – 7 AM) during which only specific apps and phone calls are allowed.
  • Communication Limits: Control who can be contacted during Screen Time and Downtime.
  • Content & Privacy Restrictions: Block explicit content, restrict purchases, and limit changes to settings.
  • Focus Modes: Reduce distractions by filtering notifications and calls. Includes Work, Personal, Sleep, and custom Focus modes.

How to Access

  1. Open Settings
  2. Tap Screen Time

🖥️ macOS

macOS Catalina (10.15) and later include Screen Time with many of the same features as iOS.

Key Features

  • Usage Reports: See time spent per app, website, and category across your Mac and connected Apple devices.
  • App Limits: Set time limits for app categories or individual apps.
  • Downtime: Schedule time away from the screen. During Downtime only allowed apps are accessible.
  • Communication Limits: Restrict who you can contact.
  • Content & Privacy: Block explicit websites and restrict access to certain system features.
  • Focus Modes: Available via System Settings → Focus — filter notifications while working, sleeping, or during custom activities.

How to Access

  1. Open System Settings (or System Preferences on older macOS)
  2. Click Screen Time

🪟 Windows

Windows 10 and 11 offer Screen Time for Family Safety accounts and a built-in Focus feature.

Focus Sessions (Windows 11)

Windows 11 includes a Focus feature in the Clock app that integrates with Microsoft To Do and optionally plays ambient music.

  • Set a focus session duration (e.g., 25 or 50 minutes)
  • Notifications are silenced automatically during sessions
  • Track daily progress with a streak counter
  1. Open the Clock app
  2. Click Focus sessions

📖 Official Windows 11 Focus guide

Microsoft Family Safety (Screen Time)

For family or personal accounts linked to a Microsoft account, Family Safety provides app and game limits, web filtering, and usage reports.

  • Set daily screen time schedules and limits per device
  • Block specific apps, games, or websites
  • Receive weekly activity reports

📖 Microsoft Family Safety guide

Do Not Disturb / Focus Assist (Windows 10)

Windows 10 includes Focus Assist to suppress notifications during specified hours or activities.

  1. Open Settings → System → Focus Assist
  2. Choose "Priority only" or "Alarms only" to limit interruptions
  3. Set automatic rules for specific times, when duplicating your display, or when playing a game

🐧 Linux

Linux distributions vary widely, but several tools are available depending on your desktop environment.

GNOME — Usage & Wellbeing

GNOME (used by Ubuntu, Fedora, and others) includes a Usage app to monitor app and network activity, and screen time scheduling via Parental Controls.

  • Install GNOME Usage: sudo apt install gnome-usage (Debian/Ubuntu)
  • Set Parental Controls via Settings → Parental Controls (GNOME 3.38+)
